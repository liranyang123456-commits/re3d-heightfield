\opening{Dear Professor Zhai,}

We transfer the manuscript
``\textbf{Uncollapsed and Metrically-Scaled 3D Reconstruction from Monocular Endoscopic Video via Pseudo-3D Heightfields}''
to \textit{Displays} as a Research Article. The manuscript asks a display-first question: can monocular endoscopic video be turned into a 3D surface that is simultaneously \emph{displayable} (un-collapsed, renderable) and \emph{measurable} (metrically scaled)? We show that the answer is a pseudo-3D heightfield used in two roles---as a geometric matching substrate and as an operator corrector.

\textbf{Why Displays.}
The useful output is a renderable mesh for intra-operative overlay and measurement, not a disparity map alone. A collapsed cone or planar slab cannot be visualized as a surgical surface and cannot be used to measure physical lengths. This addresses a fundamental display problem: visual signal processing, an un-collapsed 3D surface, and explicit metric scale recovery.

\textbf{The story in one paragraph.}
Feed-forward 3D foundation models (VGGT, DUSt3R, MASt3R, MUSt3R) produce dense but geometrically collapsed, scale-less clouds on endoscopic video, and classical SfM drifts or mis-decomposes under small planar baselines. We reconstruct a bilateral gradient-anchored pseudo-3D heightfield from each frame and exploit it twice. As a \emph{geometric matching substrate}, it registers frames (FPFH/RANSAC/ICP), builds dense epipolar correspondences, and feeds a multi-stride bundle adjustment that suppresses dead-reckoning drift. As an \emph{operator corrector}, its physical normal field vetoes the wrong homography branch, and a calibration gauge injects true metric scale. The same mesh therefore drives matching, pose, branch correction, and scale---so the surface stays three-dimensional and metric where the baselines collapse.

\textbf{Headline results.}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Un-collapsed Normal\,3D geometry:} On the endoscopic chessboard benchmark the heightfield is the only \emph{monocular} reconstruction that stays Normal\,3D ($\lambda_3/\lambda_1 = 0.42$); VGGT, MASt3R, DUSt3R, MUSt3R, COLMAP and ORB-SfM all collapse to Flat or Cone ($\leq 0.22$), and only the stereo SGM reference also reaches Normal\,3D ($0.31$).
    \item \textbf{Near-ideal metric scale:} The gauge recovers $1.11\times$ scale against the reference, while every baseline drifts by $3.7\times$--$647\times$.
    \item \textbf{Leading depth on public data:} On EndoNeRF the calibrated stereo variant attains the best AbsRel ($0.0285$), ahead of FoundationStereo ($0.0321$) and Stereo SGM ($0.0323$).
    \item \textbf{Drift suppression and gauge ceiling:} On a 17-sequence benchmark the pattern-free multi-view BA outperforms ORB-SfM ($35.52$ vs.\ $43.72$\,mm mean ATE) and beats VGGT-1B in a smoke-filled cavity ($24.86$ vs.\ $37.25$\,mm); with the gauge visible, the gauge-referenced trajectory reaches the $4.49$\,mm pattern-localisation floor.
\end{itemize}
