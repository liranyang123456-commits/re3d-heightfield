\opening{Dear Editors-in-Chief,}

We submit the manuscript
``\textbf{Pseudo-3D Heightfield Reconstruction from Monocular Endoscopic Video via Gradient-Anchored Geometric Constraints}''
as a Research Article for consideration in \textit{Computerized Medical Imaging and Graphics} (CMIG).

\textbf{Why CMIG.}
The paper addresses a computational and visualization problem in endoscopic imaging: recovering a metric, renderable 3D surface from monocular (and, when available, stereo) surgical video. Collapsed or planar reconstructions cannot support augmented-reality overlay or image-guided measurement. The proposed 2.5-D heightfield is both a geometric representation and a visualizable mesh, which matches CMIG's scope in reconstruction, visualization, endoscopy, and image-guided intervention.

\textbf{What is new.}
\begin{itemize}
    \item A shared 2.5-D surface embedding: the same heightfield is registered for pose and queried for dense matching. Replacing that pose with a 2D essential matrix collapses the cloud, isolating the embedding rather than the fusion code.
    \item Shape is treated as a first-class criterion ($\lambda_3/\lambda_1$). Calibration patterns set the metric gauge and the mask; they are not the reconstructed primitive.
    \item An optional stereo variant that keeps the heightfield as a shape regularizer (AbsRel 0.0285 on the shared EndoNeRF 100-frame set).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Evidence.}
On an endoscopic chessboard benchmark, the proposed method and Stereo SGM are the only approaches that produce Normal 3D point clouds; the learned and SfM baselines collapse to cones or planes. Replacing heightfield-ICP poses with a 2D essential-matrix front-end collapses the cloud, isolating the heightfield signal. On SCARED, a 22-sequence stereo-laparoscopic set, and EndoNeRF, the method matches Stereo SGM in depth accuracy and supplies a confidence map for visualization. Four newly captured phantom sequences (different rig, calibrated PnP reference) show the same scale advantage on two sequences and a Normal 3D cloud on one; two sequences collapse, consistent with sparse metric anchors rather than a $32^{\circ}$ rotation statistic inflated by planar-PnP flips.
